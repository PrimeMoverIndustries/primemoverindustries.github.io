About the game

January 1890. Five million dollars of your own money, an office in the state you choose, and nobody on the payroll. The electric age is beginning, and the men who will own it have not yet been decided.

Found a company. Hire scientists, engineers, draughtsmen and accountants. Stake your money at the bench, prove each claim in a field trial before a rival can, and carry what you win to the Patent Office. Build works on the land you buy, sell what they make at the price the trade will bear, and borrow from houses that remember how you treated them.

Features

  • The Laboratory. More than three hundred technologies in seven fields, from the dynamo to the computer. Every claim is proved in a field trial, with money and engineers at stake.
  • The Patent Desk. File the claim yourself, stamp, envelope and chute, until you can pay a clerk to do it.
  • The Drawing Office. Design power lines, power stations, locomotives, motor cars, airships, airliners and ships part by part; the figures on the drawing are the figures on the books.
  • The Big Board. Twenty-six concerns quoted in eighths, as the New York exchange quoted them. Ride out the panics and take your own company public.
  • The Houses. Morgan, Standard Oil, Edison, Westinghouse, Carnegie, the Patent Office and the Federal Government, with tempers and memories; some would rather beat you in court than in the market.
  • The century. The New-York Courier, the wireless and television report a story every year from 1890 to 2040, and your choices change what they print: Mr. Tesla, the ballot of 1896, the wars.
  • The country. The United States from NASA imagery and elevation data, with seasons, storms, towns that grow with the census, and lamps that come on where the wires reach.
